Part 2 · Three product concepts

Rooms, Dock, Stage

Three different answers to the same question: how should a local-file player organise finding, playing and keeping music? Each concept keeps every documented capability and is rendered with the same content, the same 390×844 frame and the same seven tasks.

Evaluation criteria and weights

Weights follow the product brief: Daria is a local player used repeatedly, often one-handed, in English and Persian, on small and large phones. Findability and listening continuity carry the most weight because every job in the brief starts with finding something already on the phone and ends with uninterrupted playback. Delight is weighted below task clarity on purpose: the brief asks for an identity that earns its cost.

Scores below are 1–5 comparative judgements by the designer. They are not user data. The weighted totals are used to rank, not to prove.

C1 · Rooms

Organising principle: three rooms for three moods. Listen (resume and revisit), Library (find deliberately), Search (find by name).

Rooms takes the strongest idea from the Figma reference and the Sessions study seriously: people who open a music app often want to continue, not browse. It gives that job its own destination with a resume card and personal shortcuts, keeps the full library as a second room, and makes global search a third. A mini player sits above the navigation bar. Now Playing is a full-screen sheet; Queue is a modal sheet on top of it.

Product / IA map

Essential interaction rules

  • Bottom bar: single selection, always visible except inside Now Playing and editors. Re-tapping the current room scrolls to top.
  • Listen shows a resume card only when a session exists; with no history it shows shortcuts and “Recently added”. Nothing is generated or recommended.
  • Mini player: tap anywhere opens Now Playing; play/pause and next are direct.
  • Back inside Now Playing returns to the room that opened it; Back on a room returns to Listen; Back on Listen leaves the app.
  • Queue is a modal sheet (32% scrim, drag handles). Transport is hidden under it; the sheet must close to seek.
  • Settings lives behind the gear on Listen. It also carries a “Listen page” toggle to hide history, because the history room is a preference some people will not want.

Strongest advantages

  • Resume and revisit are one tap from launch; this is the largest reduction in taps for repeat listening of the three.
  • Global search is a first-class destination with its own recents, which the current app lacks.
  • Familiar three-tab model; lowest learning cost; fits the Figma annotations and the design system.

Actual compromises

  • Two persistent bands (mini player + nav) consume about 130 px on every browsing screen; at 160% text the list shows five rows on a 320 px phone.
  • Listen must be filled with content from a personal library. With a small or new library it is thin; with a huge library it repeats what the smart collections already offer.
  • Queue as a modal sheet hides the transport, which is the same limitation the current app has 34.
  • Settings is reachable only from one room, which is one more thing to remember.

Greatest uncertainty

Whether a home room earns its place in an offline library app. The Sessions study asked the same question and nothing in the package answers it. This is the concept to test if the owner believes “continue listening” is the dominant job.

C2 · Dock

Organising principle: one library, one dock. Everything you browse is above; everything you touch most is in a dock at your thumb.

Dock removes the top-level navigation question by having a single library with five lenses, and moves the two most frequent global actions, search and playback, into a dock at the bottom of every browsing screen. Now Playing is a full-screen surface with three faces (Cover, Lyrics, Up next) that share one fixed transport, so the queue is never a modal that hides play/pause. Settings is a gear on the library header and is the only place library rules live, but every place the rules bite (counts, playlists, search) shows a pill or footer that explains and links back.

Product / IA map

Essential interaction rules

  • Lens choice is remembered. At large text or below 360 px the lens row becomes a labelled picker, never a clipped strip.
  • The dock is present on library, collection, playlist, settings and info screens. It hides under the tag editor and the add-songs picker, where the primary button takes its place.
  • Tapping the search field opens Search; the field stays docked above the keyboard so results and typing are both reachable one-handed.
  • Now Playing faces are tabs, not swipes. Swipe is an optional accelerator; the tabs are the accessible path.
  • Back: closes overlays first (system UI, dialog, sheet, player), then pops the screen stack, then returns to Songs, then leaves the app. A dirty editor or a non-empty selection asks before discarding.
  • Every rule that hides content (folders, minimum length) is explained where it is felt: the pill on counts, the footer in playlists and favourites, the hint under empty search results.

Strongest advantages

  • Search and playback are always one thumb-length away, on every screen, without a second persistent band.
  • Queue editing never hides play/pause or the timeline; the queue is a face of the player, not a modal over it.
  • Hidden-content rules are explained where they bite, which makes the folder-visibility contract (files, playlists and queue preserved) legible rather than mysterious.
  • Single root means Back behaviour is simple and predictable.

Actual compromises

  • No dedicated “continue listening” page. Resume is the mini player; recently played is a tile under Playlists. If the owner wants a history-first experience this is weaker than Rooms.
  • The dock is a new composition; the search field costs 48 px on browsing screens and needs a solid fallback where blur is expensive.
  • Three faces in Now Playing put Lyrics (a future feature) in view before it exists. The prototype shows the empty state honestly; the risk is that it looks like a broken feature until lyrics ship.

Greatest uncertainty

Whether people discover that the mini player opens the full player when the search field sits directly under it, and whether the Up next face is found without a “Queue” label on the cover face. Both are cheap to test and cheap to fix (a label under the chip; a first-run hint).

C3 · Stage

Organising principle: the player is the app. Playback is always on stage; the library is a sheet you raise over it.

Stage inverts the usual hierarchy. The app opens on a persistent player that shows artwork, transport, a horizontal “Up next” strip and a library sheet resting at the bottom. Raising the sheet compacts the stage into a slim bar with the waveform and play button; the sheet holds search, the five lenses and every management screen. There is no separate Now Playing screen to open or close and no mini player: the stage is the player at every size.

Product / IA map

Essential interaction rules

  • Two sheet positions only: resting (stage full, four rows visible) and raised (stage compact). A tap on the handle toggles; drag is an accelerator.
  • Compact stage keeps play/pause, the waveform and title. Tapping it lowers the sheet.
  • Up next strip scrolls horizontally; “Edit” opens a vertical queue list inside the sheet with drag handles and multi-select.
  • Lyrics (future) and Up next are faces of the sheet, alongside Library, when the stage is full.
  • Back: lowers a raised sheet before popping any screen inside it. From resting position Back leaves the app.
  • With nothing playing, the stage shows the last session paused, or on first run a “Pick a song” stage with the sheet raised.

Strongest advantages

  • Strongest identity of the three; artwork and colour lead every screen, which is where the owner asked for expressiveness.
  • Playback is never more than visible; there is no mini/full split to learn and no transition to wait for.
  • The Up next strip makes the queue visible without opening anything.

Actual compromises

  • Browsing gets the smaller half of the screen; deliberate library work (large libraries, long lists, folder management) happens in a sheet with four to six rows visible at 100% text and fewer at 160%.
  • Sheet drag competes with list scroll, queue reorder and edge-back gestures; the tap-to-toggle fallback makes it usable but the two-position model still has to be learned.
  • Landscape needs its own composition; a horizontal strip plus a sheet does not fit 390 px of height.
  • Missing artwork makes the stage look empty for much of a file-derived library (24 of the 41 captured rows use fallback initials today).

Greatest uncertainty

Whether people with large, messy libraries accept browsing in a half-height sheet. The 825-song library on Hormoz, with 718 files in one Quick Share folder, is exactly the case this concept serves worst.

Recommendation: develop Dock, keep Stage’s stage as an experiment

Dock is recommended. It wins on the two heaviest criteria because it removes a decision (which room am I in?) rather than adding a surface, and because the two most frequent actions in a large local library, searching and controlling playback, are within thumb reach on every screen. It preserves every contract without a modal queue and explains the one new rule (hidden content) everywhere the rule is felt. Its weaknesses are testable and cheap to fix: discoverability of the player faces and the absence of a history-first page.

What Dock borrows, with sources. A direct Library entry and lens row (current app, Round 2). Search as a global, always-present affordance (Figma F05 makes it a tab; Dock docks it instead). Queue reachable without leaving transport (a reaction to Figma F03 and capture 34, which both cover the transport). The end-of-song timer status in the mini player (capture 27). The folder rule explanation (Round 2 settings copy, rewritten). None of these is adopted because it is precedent; each is adopted because it scored better against a criterion above.

What is kept for later. Rooms’ Listen page is not needed to ship Dock; if usage or the owner shows that resume-and-revisit dominates, a “Listening” lens or a resume card at the top of Songs can be added without changing the architecture. Stage’s persistent player is the most expressive idea in this set; it is proposed as a validation experiment (a “Stage” mode reachable from Now Playing) rather than the default, because it costs browsing density and feasibility for a library like the one captured.

Owner decisions required before build. (1) Accept a single-root library with no Home. (2) Accept that Lyrics appears as a face with an honest empty state before the feature exists, or hold the face behind a flag until lyrics ship. (3) Confirm the hidden-content rule: hidden songs remain in playlists, favourites, history and the queue, are listed under a “hidden” footer, and can be played from there. (4) Confirm “Save queue as playlist” as a new capability, or drop it.
