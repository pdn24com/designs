Strengths worth preserving

The contracts are already careful

The sleep timer distinguishes a draft from an active deadline, rejects 181 minutes with a reason and keeps the running timer on “Keep current” Observed 21–23. The editor says it rewrites the actual file and that Android may ask Observed 32. Delete and Clear both say the songs stay on the phone 09, 35. The redesign keeps these semantics and their copy structure.

Direct entry into the library

The app opens on Songs with the count, sort and shuffle visible and a persistent mini player Observed 01. Round 2 kept this for a reason: it is the shortest route to a person’s files. No reference proves a home page would be shorter.

Recovery vocabulary exists

Undo after playlist deletion 10, a query-specific no-match state 39, a clear-queue confirmation 35, invalid-input feedback 22. The contracts add retained-catalogue refresh failure and draft-preserving save errors Contract. These are the seeds of a consistent recovery system.

Song info is honest about sources

“From the file’s tags (ID3v2.3)”, “Not in this file: Album artist, Track number, Disc, Genre”, plus file and audio facts Observed 29–30. This is rare in players and worth making more visible.

Opportunities

  • One action vocabulary. Play now / Play next / Add to queue / Add to playlist / Favourite / Song info / Share, grouped by consequence (this session, keep, go to, file), identical in every menu.
  • Explain hidden content where it is felt. A single “n hidden” affordance that appears on counts, playlists and empty search, opening one sheet that lists why and offers one-tap recovery. This turns the folder invariant into something people can see.
  • A player with faces instead of sheets. Cover, Lyrics and Up next as tabs of one player with one transport; the queue stops being a modal.
  • A Daria fallback tile. A generated, per-album tinted tile replaces initials, so a library without embedded art still looks like a library.
  • Source-aware titles. A subtle “from file name” marker in Song info and in the editor, with a “file name” badge in rows only when the person opts in (default off to keep rows quiet).
  • Settings and Lyrics as clearly future. Both designed in full, with first-release scopes that need no network, no rights agreement and no new data source.

The product questions that matter most, answered provisionally

These are the decisions with the largest effect on the experience. Each is developed in the decision register with alternatives, costs and what would change my mind.

Q1 What is the first useful screen?

The Songs lens, with the last session in the dock. A home page needs content the product does not generate; the resume job is served by the mini player.

Q2 Does the main experience need a navigation bar?

No. Five lenses under one root plus a dock give every destination one tap without a second persistent band.

Q3 Where does search live?

In the dock, on every browsing screen, and above the keyboard when open.

Q4 Where does the queue live?

Inside the player as a face, never as a modal over the transport.

Q5 Should Add be one action?

No. Queue actions are session-scoped and reversible in seconds; playlist membership persists. They share a sheet but keep separate verbs and groups.

Q6 Favourites versus playlists?

Favourites is a switch, not a playlist row, so it cannot be confused with a destination that has membership counts.

Q7 Where does Settings live and what is immediate?

A gear on the library header. Every setting applies immediately with Undo; the number each option hides is shown before choosing. No Apply/Discard draft.

Q8 How are hidden songs explained?

One amber pill wherever counts change, one sheet that lists the reasons, one tap to show a folder again. Hidden songs remain in playlists and the queue and are listed under an expandable footer.

Q9 What is Lyrics in v1?

Lyrics embedded in the file (synced or plain) and a per-song lyrics file the person chooses. No online source, no matching, no permissions beyond audio access.

Q10 What can a static mockup not tell us?

Whether faces are discovered, whether the dock’s blur is affordable, whether TalkBack order is right. Each is in the validation plan.

Open questions, conflicts and assumptions

Conflicts between sources

  • Unknown duration. Round 2 kept unknown-duration tracks as a fixed rule Proposal; the brief says this is not approved Owner. Resolved as a default (kept) with a switch, and unknown duration is never treated as zero Contract.
  • What folder hiding preserves. The owner invariant preserves files, authored playlists and the queue; Round 2 additionally preserved favourites and history Proposal. This design preserves favourites and history too and marks it as requiring an owner decision (D7).
  • Timer placement. The current app has three placements; Round 2 has one bottom action; Figma has none. This design uses one chip in the player and one glyph in the mini player.
  • Palette. Figma base frames are pale green; Rounds 1–2 use blue. Neither is mandated. This design uses a lapis blue accent for controls and derives the player tint from artwork, with the blue as fallback.

Assumptions made to proceed

  • A1 The 825-song count already reflects the shipped 30 s filter (flows.md reports 825 as the baseline of the installed build).
  • A2 Folder names in the library come from Android’s media index and are limited to folders containing audio.
  • A3 The shared design system can render blur on a single dock surface; a solid variant exists.
  • A4 Persian is the second locale; Arabic-Indic digit input applies to timer and numeric tag fields Contract.
  • A5 The media session (notification, lock screen, Bluetooth) is unchanged by this redesign.

Questions only the owner can answer

  • Whether hidden songs may be played from a playlist footer (this design says yes, because membership is preserved; it could be view-only).
  • Whether “Save queue as playlist” is wanted (new capability).
  • Whether the Lyrics face may ship visible with an honest empty state before lyrics exist, or should stay behind a flag.
  • Whether the app name in the header should read “Daria” or “Daria Music” (the captures use “Daria Music”; the mark here uses the note glyph plus “Daria” for width).
